# Q2-62: What's Digital Signature?

- It is a cryptographic mechanism used to verify the authenticity and integrity of digital messages or documents.

- It acts like an electronic fingerprint that confirms the origin and ensures that the message has not been altered in transit.

- Key Features:

    1. Authentication:
        
        - It verifies that the message or document was sent by the claimed sender.

    2. Integrity:
        
        - It ensures that the message or document has not been altered.

    3. Non-repudiation:

        - The sender cannot deny having signed the document or sent the message once it has been digitally signed.

- Steps:

    1. The sender creates a hash of the message or document.

    2. The hash of the message is then encrypted using the sender's private key. This encrypted hash is the digital signature.

    3. The recipient of the message uses the sender's public key to decrypt the digital signature and get the original hash.
    
    4. The recipient then hashes the received message or document. If the two hashes match, it means the document is authentic and hasn't been tampered with.

- Use cases:

    - Email
    - Legal Documents
    - Blockchain: To verify transactions
