# Q4-13: Which one is better? Business-Friendly or Generic Repository Methods?

- In most real-world applications, business-friendly repository methods are preferred over generic repositories because they express the actual use case, reduce coupling, and align better with the domain.
- Generic repositories may seem DRY, but they often lead to leaky abstractions and poor readability.

- Advantages of Business-Friendly Methods:

  - More readable.
  - Tailored to the use case.
  - Easier to test and maintain.
  - Aligned with DDD (Domain-Driven Design) — your code reflects your business language.
  - Helps avoid leaky abstractions (where your data layer leaks into your business logic).

- When Generic Repositories Are OK:

  - Small/simple CRUD-based applications.
  - You truly need reusable logic (e.g., shared logic for auditing, paging).

- Why Generic Repositories Often Fail in Practice:

  - You still need custom queries, so you start breaking your abstraction.
  - You might expose IQueryable, which can lead to inefficient or unsafe queries in the business layer.
  - They don’t express intent — they’re just infrastructure.
