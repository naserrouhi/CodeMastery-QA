# Q4-16: In Entity Framework, which collection type should I use for one-to-many navigation properties: ICollection<T>, List<T>, or IEnumerable<T>?

Excellent question — and one that actually matters in real-world EF Core apps.

✅ Short Answer
For one-to-many navigation properties in EF Core:

✅ Use ICollection<T> — this is the recommended and most flexible option.

🧠 Why ICollection<T>?
🔹 EF Core needs to:
Add and remove items from your collection (like when tracking changes, lazy loading, etc.)

So it needs read/write access, not just read-only.

🔹 ICollection<T> provides:
.Add(), .Remove(), and supports iteration (foreach)

This makes it ideal for EF’s internal tracking and change detection

🔍 What about List<T>?
You can use List<T>, and EF will work fine — in fact, EF will populate a List<T> normally.

But:

List<T> ties your entity to a specific implementation

ICollection<T> is more flexible and allows easier testing or mocking

So:
✔️ EF works with both,
✅ But ICollection<T> is the best practice (cleaner, more abstract, future-proof)

❌ What about IEnumerable<T>?
Don’t use IEnumerable<T> for navigation properties.

Why?

It’s read-only — no .Add() or .Remove()

EF can’t update it or track changes properly

Can lead to runtime issues (especially with lazy loading, change tracking, or inserts)

🧠 Interview-ready answer
For one-to-many navigation properties in EF Core, you should use ICollection<T>.
It gives EF the ability to add and remove items and supports change tracking.
List<T> also works but is less flexible. Avoid IEnumerable<T> because it’s read-only and not suitable for tracking changes.
